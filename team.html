How to read all this

The rating

Every team carries a power score out of 100, where 50 is an average team in this division, and a rating on a goals scale — the margin it would be expected to win by against an average team. Both come from the same place as the league-wide page: a blend of a win-loss model and a goal-margin model, with the league’s own caps applied, so running the score up past four goals earns nothing. The full explanation is on the rankings page.

Turning a projection into odds

That spread is measured, not assumed, and it is measured honestly: every game the league has played is re-predicted from a model fitted without that game. Scored against games it had already seen, the model looks about a goal sharper than it is.

The simulation

Ratings are held still for the whole run: the model is not allowed to learn from the games it is inventing, which would let one imagined blowout compound into a runaway season. Tables are ordered the way the league orders them — points, then goal difference, then goals for, then goals against. Head-to-head is the league’s first tie-break, but it only settles a clean two-team tie, so it is left out here and a finish decided that deep should be treated as a coin flip rather than a forecast.

What it does not know

There is no home-field adjustment, no knowledge of who is away on a given weekend, who is playing up, and no weather. A rescheduled game is assumed to be played on the new date the league posted for it. Treat a gap of a point or two as noise, and treat everything here as a description of the record so far rather than a promise about a Sunday afternoon.
