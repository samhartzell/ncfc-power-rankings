How this is calculated

Two ratings, blended

Every team gets two independent ratings, then they are combined into the Power score on a 0–100 scale where 50 is an average team in that division.

Colley looks only at wins and losses. Every team starts at .500, and a team’s rating is pulled toward the average rating of the opponents it has faced. Beat a good team and your rating rises more than it would for beating a bad one.

Massey looks at goal margin, so a 4–0 win counts for more than a 1–0 win over the same opponent. It is solved as a single system across the whole division, which means a margin is judged against the opponent who conceded it.

Blowouts are capped, because the league caps them

This league records a maximum of four goals per team per game, and caps each game’s differential at four. Those caps are applied here too, so beating a weak team 9–0 is worth exactly the same as beating them 4–0. Running up the score earns nothing.

Strength of schedule

SOS is the average power score of the opponents a team has actually played. A team with SOS rank 1 has faced the hardest slate in its division so far. Early in a season this varies a lot, which is most of the reason the power order and the table order differ.

What the numbers mean

Rating is on a goals scale: it is the margin a team would be expected to win by against an average team in its division. The gap between two ratings is a projected margin, which is where the “Next up” calls come from.

Read it with the sample size in mind

There is no home-field adjustment. These are club fields with short travel, and a sample this small could not separate a real home effect from noise. The model also has no knowledge of who was missing, who is playing up, or what the weather did.
